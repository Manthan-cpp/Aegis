## Section 63 — rape

Section 63 defines rape by reference to specified sexual acts carried out
against a woman’s will, without consent, through fear or hurt, when she cannot
communicate consent, or in other circumstances listed in the section. The
complete statutory wording and exceptions must be read before applying it to
facts.

## Sections 74–76 — criminal force, sexual harassment and disrobing

Section 74 addresses assault or criminal force against a woman with intent to
outrage her modesty. Section 75 addresses sexual harassment, including listed
unwelcome sexual conduct. Section 76 addresses assault or criminal force with
intent to disrobe a woman. The exact elements and punishments are set out in
the statutory text.

## Sections 77–79 — voyeurism, stalking and insulting modesty

Sections 77, 78 and 79 address voyeurism, stalking, and words, gestures or acts
intended to insult the modesty of a woman. Which provision is relevant depends
on the conduct and evidence in the individual case.

## Section 80 — dowry death

Section 80 sets out the offence of dowry death and contains the statutory
conditions and punishment. The complete section and its explanations must be
consulted before drawing any conclusion about a specific death or investigation.

## Section 85 — cruelty by husband or relative

Section 85 provides that a husband or a relative of the husband who subjects a
woman to cruelty may be punished with imprisonment for a term that may extend
to three years and may also be liable to fine.

## Section 86 — meaning of cruelty for section 85

For section 85, “cruelty” includes wilful conduct likely to drive a woman to
commit suicide or cause grave injury or danger to life, limb, or health,
including mental or physical health. It also includes harassment where the
harassment is intended to coerce the woman or a person related to her to meet an
unlawful demand for property or valuable security, or is connected with failure
to meet that demand.

## Sections 34–40 — private defence of the body

Section 34 states that an act done in the exercise of the right of private
defence is not an offence. Section 35 recognises a right, subject to the
restrictions in section 37, to defend one’s own body or another person’s body
against an offence affecting the human body.

Section 37 limits that right. There is no right of private defence where there
is time to seek protection from public authorities, and the right does not
extend to inflicting more harm than is necessary for the purpose of defence.
The facts, immediacy of the threat, and proportionality matter.

Section 38 describes circumstances in which private defence of the body may
extend to causing death, including an assault that reasonably creates an
apprehension of death or grievous hurt, an assault with intent to commit rape,
an assault with intent to kidnap or abduct, or an assault intended to wrongfully
confine a person in circumstances where access to public authorities may be
unavailable. Section 39 covers cases where the right extends to harm other than
death, and section 40 addresses when the right begins and continues.

These provisions do not create a general permission to attack someone because
of earlier abuse, anger, revenge, or a fear that is not immediate. Whether a
particular act was lawful self-defence is fact-specific and must be assessed by
a qualified lawyer or court.

## Important limitation

The BNS provisions are criminal-law provisions. A chat answer cannot determine
whether the legal elements of an offence are met, identify an accused person,
or predict an investigation or court result.
