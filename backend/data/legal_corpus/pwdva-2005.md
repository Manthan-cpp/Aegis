## Section 2 — selected definitions

The Act defines an “aggrieved person” as a woman who is, or has been, in a
domestic relationship with the respondent and who alleges that she has been
subjected to domestic violence. A “domestic relationship” includes people who
live or have lived together in a shared household when related by marriage,
relationship in the nature of marriage, adoption, or as family members living
together as a joint family.

## Section 3 — domestic violence

Domestic violence includes physical abuse, sexual abuse, verbal and emotional
abuse, and economic abuse. The Act also covers conduct that harms, injures,
endangers, or has the effect of harming the physical or mental well-being of the
aggrieved person, including coercion connected with unlawful demands for dowry
or other property. The statutory text should be read for the complete
definitions and explanations.

## Section 5 — duties of police officers, service providers and Magistrate

When a police officer, protection officer, service provider, or Magistrate
receives a complaint of domestic violence, the Act sets out duties including
informing the aggrieved person about available reliefs, services of protection
officers and service providers, and the availability of free legal services
under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.

## Section 12 — application to Magistrate

An aggrieved person, a protection officer, or another person on her behalf may
present an application to the Magistrate seeking one or more reliefs under the
Act. The application is made in the prescribed manner and jurisdictional rules
apply.

## Section 17 — right to reside in a shared household

Every woman in a domestic relationship has a right to reside in the shared
household, whether or not she has a right, title, or beneficial interest in it.
The Act says that an aggrieved person must not be evicted or excluded from the
shared household by the respondent except according to the procedure
established by law.

## Section 18 — protection orders

After giving the aggrieved person and respondent an opportunity to be heard and
being prima facie satisfied that domestic violence has happened or is likely to
happen, the Magistrate may prohibit the respondent from acts such as committing
or aiding domestic violence, entering the aggrieved person’s workplace or other
places she frequents, attempting personal/electronic/telephone communication,
dealing with specified joint or individual assets without the Magistrate’s
leave, or causing violence to dependants, relatives, or people assisting her.
The exact order depends on the proceeding and the statutory conditions.

## Section 19 — residence orders

Where the Magistrate is satisfied that domestic violence has taken place, a
residence order may restrain dispossession from the shared household, direct the
respondent to leave it in the circumstances permitted by the Act, restrict entry
to the portion where the aggrieved person resides, restrain disposal or
encumbrance of the shared household, or require alternate accommodation or rent
where the circumstances require. The Act contains important conditions and
exceptions, including one concerning an order to remove a woman from the shared
household.

## Sections 20–23 — other selected reliefs

Section 20 concerns monetary reliefs. Section 21 concerns temporary custody
orders. Section 22 concerns compensation orders. Section 23 allows the
Magistrate to grant interim and ex parte orders where the statutory conditions
are met.

## Sections 26–28 — related proceedings and procedure

Section 26 addresses relief in other suits and legal proceedings. Section 27
sets out jurisdiction. Section 28 describes procedure under the Act and rules
made under it, subject to the statutory text and applicable court practice.

## Important limitation

This summary is for retrieval and orientation only. It does not determine
whether a person qualifies for a relief, which court has jurisdiction, or what
steps a lawyer should recommend in an individual matter.
